I classify solitaire boardgame types as follows:

1. Mission-complete, like Fantasy Flight LCGs. These sometimes include stories or campaigns. But if it's a solo version of a co-op game, player-count scaling can be a problem, and you may need to play two or more hands.

2. High-score game, like Agricola. This is the most straightforward design, since in many games "winning" just means "playing better than your opponent." There might be minor tweaks to this, but mostly it's just running a multiplayer game alone. Most people don't like this design, because the goal isn't clear even when there's a win threshold like "get 100 points and you win." Personally, I don't even count it as a "valid solitaire."

3. Heavy Automa, like the Imperium series. These often use flowcharts, cards, and more. They mimic a human player with complex decisions. The interaction feels real, but setting up, reading, and doing everything for the virtual opponent isn't the fun part, and it sometimes takes more time than my own turn.

4. Normal Automa, like The White Castle. The boundary isn't clear, but to me some automas feel "normal weight." Each turn, they draw just one card, do their thing, and are done. A very simple mimic of a human player, but the cost is that they're sometimes too simple or too strong.

5. Weak Automa, like Viticulture, the Castle of Burgundy card version, or Al Cabohne. They behave nothing like a human. They're just a proper blocker for a worker-placement game (Viticulture), or they take a single turn while I play several and set an objective score (the Castle of Burgundy card version), or they simply steal my resources and set the target score (Al Cabohne). They implement one slice of a human player, taking a single role.

I have a clear preference among these five types. Sometimes I really love a game but don't play it, or am reluctant to, because it has no solo mode, or:

1. It's a mission-complete game, but it scales poorly in solo.
2. It's a high-score game. I just don't play this type, with the only exception of Nusfjord.
3. The Heavy Automa is too heavy and costly.

I'm fine with types 4 (Normal) and 5 (Weak) automa normally, but one more thing: if the score calculation is too much, that's another drawback.

As a solo player, I've made tons of solitaire variants ever since I was a kid, and now it's time to share them. So this Q-Project begins. There seem to be many good names for such bots; mine is just Q. No further reason. Maybe just because I'm queer.

The main objectives of this project are:

1. For mission games, address the scaling problem.
2. Provide clear alternative goals.
3. Reproduce human interaction within one card per round, or less.
